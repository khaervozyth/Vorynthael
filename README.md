# Vorynthael

> The candle cast a shadow before anyone brought it into the room.

## The Chamber of Unlit Gold

At the northern edge of an unnamed kingdom stands a house with no northern wall. Snow enters through the missing stonework, yet the floor remains warm. Travelers describe the house as abandoned. Travelers who spend the night there describe it as occupied by someone who has not arrived.

The chamber contains a candle, a bowl of black sand, and a chair facing the open weather. None of these objects appears in the inventories left by the household. Each inventory mentions a fourth object, written in a script that fades when held near a flame.

Vorynthael is the word carved beneath the chair. The carving is visible only when the chair is occupied. No witness has managed to explain how it was first discovered.

## The Visitor with Silver Teeth

A visitor came during a winter without frost. Its body resembled a dragon, but its wings were folded beneath its scales, visible as pale movements under dark stone. Its teeth were silver. Between them, the household could hear the footsteps of people walking through distant rooms.

The visitor asked for an empty bowl. The keeper offered the bowl of black sand. The creature accepted it without spilling a grain, then placed something invisible upon the table.

By morning, the table had become cold enough to preserve a breath. The keeper's final words remained suspended above it for eleven days. Nobody remembered speaking those words. They were addressed to a child who would be born much later, in a village that had not yet been founded.

## A Letter Written Inside the Fold

The keeper left a letter folded into a triangle. Its outer surface carried a single instruction: open it only when the chair faces away from the weather.

Several generations obeyed. The chair never moved. Then a mason entered the chamber with a broken compass, and everyone present agreed that the open wall was no longer north. They unfolded the letter.

Inside, there was a drawing of the same chamber, viewed from a place behind its missing wall. The candle was burning. The bowl was empty. The chair held a figure whose face had been removed from the paper without damaging its surface.

On the reverse, a sentence appeared in the keeper's handwriting: the guest has returned what the house could not carry.

The mason repaired his compass. The drawing lost its candle.

## The Measure of a Breath

Scholars arrived with instruments, scales, and polished mirrors. They measured the table, counted the grains of sand, and examined the chair. Their observations agreed until they attempted to measure the distance between the candle and its shadow.

The distance increased whenever someone spoke. It decreased whenever someone dreamed. After three nights, one scholar found the shadow in his own bedroom, seated beside a candle he had never purchased.

He returned to the chamber carrying the unfamiliar candle. It would not fit through the doorway, although it was smaller than his hand. The household suggested bringing it through the missing wall. He refused, saying that something on the other side had already learned its shape.

His instruments remain in a locked cupboard. The cupboard casts no shadow during winter.

## Words Beneath the Sand

**Vor — yn — thael — ekh.**

The four sounds were recovered from the bowl after the visitor departed. They were not written upon the grains. Rather, the sand formed shallow hollows, and each hollow spoke when someone leaned close enough to hear the room behind their own breathing.

The first listener heard a greeting. The second heard the name of a vanished river. The third heard nothing and began answering questions before they were asked.

The household covered the bowl with cloth. By morning, the cloth bore a map of the house with four northern walls. Beyond each wall stood the same silver-toothed visitor, holding a different empty bowl.

Nobody could decide which house the map depicted. Nobody could explain why every doorway was drawn from the outside.

## The Keeper's Unfinished Vigil

> If the candle burns blue, leave the chair empty.
>
> If the sand becomes white, speak to the open wall.
>
> If the visitor asks for fire, remember the breath upon the table.

These instructions were discovered on the underside of the cupboard. A fourth line had been concealed beneath a hinge. When the hinge was removed, the household found no words there, only an impression resembling a closed eye.

That night, every sleeper dreamed of the same cupboard opening inward. Behind it stood a room without weather, where an unoccupied chair faced a solid wall. Something was knocking from the other side, very slowly, with the patience of falling snow.

## Where the Missing Wall Begins

The house still stands. Its candle remains unlit, though the shadow crosses the floor at different hours. The household keeps the bowl covered and the chair facing the direction their instruments refuse to name.

Sometimes a traveler arrives carrying a letter folded into a triangle. Sometimes the silver teeth are visible in the reflection of a window far from the chamber. Neither event appears in the inventories.

Vorynthael waits beneath the occupied chair. The fourth object continues fading wherever a flame is brought too close.

*The room remembers the weight of an unspoken breath.*
